Most homes in Vietnam are lit by one source: a ceiling fixture in the middle of the room. It is bright enough, but it flattens every surface and takes away the depth of the room after dark.

## Three basic layers

**Ambient light** is the soft, indirect layer, usually washing from a ceiling cove or along the skirting. It gives you enough light to move around without seeing the source.

**Task light** serves one job: a strip over the worktop, a reading lamp by the chair, mirror lights in the bathroom.

**Accent light** is for what you want to look at: a painting, a stone surface, a shelf of books.

![Three layers of light in a living room: ambient light from a ceiling cove, task light from a floor lamp, accent light on the shelves](/images/journal-anh-sang-ba-lop-1600.webp)

## Control each layer separately

What matters is not how many lamps you have but that each layer has its own switch. For a film in the evening you only need the ambient layer, dimmed. With guests, add the accents. One room, three atmospheres.

![The same living room in three lighting scenes: watching a film, having guests, and everyday living](/images/journal-anh-sang-dieu-khien-1600.webp)

## Choosing colour temperature

For homes we almost always use warm light, 2700K – 3000K, in living rooms and bedrooms. Cool white light dulls timber and is unkind to skin.

![The same living room under warm 2700K – 3000K light and under cool white light of 4000K and above](/images/journal-anh-sang-nhiet-do-mau-1600.webp)

If you could change only one thing in your current home, add a floor lamp in the corner and switch the ceiling light off. You will notice the difference that same evening.
