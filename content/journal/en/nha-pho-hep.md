title: Five ways to bring daylight into a narrow townhouse

The Vietnamese townhouse usually has one open face. The middle and rear of the house are often dark and stuffy as a result. Here are five solutions proven across many projects.

## 1. Put the lightwell in the middle, not at the back

A lightwell at the rear rescues a single room. Placed in the middle it lights both sides and creates cross-ventilation with the street front.

## 2. Open-riser stairs

The staircase is the largest light-blocking mass in a tube house. Open risers and a slender balustrade let light from the lightwell fall all the way to the ground floor.

## 3. Step the facade back in exchange for a courtyard

Give up a metre and a half of depth for a planted buffer at the front. The room behind becomes far cooler and more private.

## 4. Partitions that stop short of the ceiling

Between rooms that do not need complete privacy, a glazed band above the partition lets light travel deeper.

## 5. Light floors and ceilings; walls can be dark

Light bounces mainly off the floor and ceiling. Keep those two bright and you are free to use deep colours on the walls without darkening the house.

Every plot has a different sun path and different neighbours, so there is no single formula. But giving up a little floor area in exchange for daylight is almost always the right decision.
